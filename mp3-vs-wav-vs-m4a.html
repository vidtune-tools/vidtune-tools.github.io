MP3 vs WAV vs M4A

Three common audio formats, and which one fits what you're doing.

All three store sound, but they make different trade-offs between quality, size and compatibility.

MP3

The most compatible format. It plays on nearly every phone, car, speaker and app. It's lossy: some detail is discarded to save space. At 192 to 320 kbps most listeners can't tell the difference from the original.

M4A (AAC)

A newer compressed format. At the same file size it usually sounds a little better than MP3, and it's what Apple uses for Voice Memos and iTunes purchases. Support is good on Apple and modern Android devices, but older players may not open it.

WAV

Uncompressed audio. Nothing is thrown away, so it's the standard for editing and archiving. The cost is size: about ten times a 128 kbps MP3.

Which should you pick?

  • Sharing or listening on any device: MP3.
  • Best quality for the size on Apple devices: M4A.
  • Editing, mixing or archiving: WAV.
  • Speech and podcasts: MP3 at 128 kbps.

Can you convert between them?

Yes, with the audio converter. Converting from a lossy format (MP3 or M4A) to WAV doesn't bring back lost detail, but it stops further loss while you edit. Going from WAV to a lossy format is where you save space.

Quick answers

Does WAV sound better than MP3?
WAV keeps everything, so it's technically better, but at 320 kbps most people can't hear the difference.
Is M4A better than MP3?
Usually slightly, at the same size, but MP3 is more widely supported.
Which format is best for podcasts?
MP3 at 128 kbps is the common choice.
